SUE — walk-through & storytelling examples

A slower, static companion to the interactive Semantic Universe Explorer. Five direct competitors in semiconductor-manufacturing equipment (Applied Materials, ASML, KLA, Lam Research, and TEL) all publish Global Impact / ESG reports every year. This page is the guided tour of what the geometry of their reports actually looks like.

What each color mode means

Company
Every dot colored by which of the five firms authored the underlying passage. Useful for eyeballing whether any single firm produces a visually distinct region of the space.
Prose ↔ tabular score
A number for each chunk from “very narrative” (blue) through zero (mixed) to “very tabular” (red). Computed as the projection onto the axis that connects the average narrative-chunk location to the average tabular-chunk location. The direction that the fixed classifier would read as “numbers vs. words.”
Digit density (raw)
The unlearned control: what percentage of the chunk’s characters are numerals. Purples = more numbers. Whenever the prose↔tabular score and digit density paint the same picture, that’s your sign that the encoder’s “semantics” on this axis is really just numerals.
Cross-corpus outlierness
For every chunk, how far is it from the average of the OTHER four competitors’ embeddings? Red = far. This is the answer to “which passages of firm X are doing something the other four aren’t?”
In-doc typicality
For every chunk, how far is it from the center of its own document? Yellow = close to the doc’s center of gravity, viridis-blue = far. Useful for spotting chunks that read very unlike the rest of their own report.
Unsupervised cluster (GMM)
A 2-component Gaussian mixture fit on the top-20 principal components, without ever being told about prose or tables. If it recovers the same partition anyway, that’s evidence the split is real.

Two firms, one sentence: cross-competitor near-twins

These are the most compelling examples in the corpus for a non-technical reader. Two direct competitors, different reports, and the sentence encoder puts their passages almost on top of each other. The content is nearly identical; only the letterhead is different.
